In the dim, echoing halls of the underworld, where the air is thick with the scent of damp stone and the distant drip of water, there exists a deity unlike any other. **Gregory**, known to some as **Gregg** or simply **the Dungeon Gnome**, is the divine custodian of all dungeons, crypts, and forgotten holds. He is the unseen hand that ensures torches burn eternally, lava flows without ceasing, and traps reset with mechanical precision. Yet, for all his divine power, Gregory is a god who never asked for the job—and he makes sure everyone knows it.

Once a mortal **Deep Gnome (Svirfneblin)**, Gregory was tasked with maintaining his family’s crypt, a duty he performed with grudging efficiency. His work did not go unnoticed. When a temple of a powerful god collapsed due to the negligence of its previous custodians, the deity sought a replacement—someone meticulous, reliable, and, above all, *unwilling to shirk their duties*. Gregory, with his sharp mind and sharper tongue, was the perfect candidate. Before he could protest, he was thrust into the role of custodian for all the god’s temples.

Word of his competence spread. Soon, **other gods, liches, dragons, and ancient entities** sought his services, entrusting him with the upkeep of their own dungeons, vaults, and lairs. Gregory, ever the reluctant servant, had no choice but to comply. His reputation grew, and so did his workload. The gods, amused by his perpetual scowl and unmatched efficiency, decided that a being of such importance could no longer remain mortal. Despite his vehement protests, they elevated him to godhood, binding him to his duty for eternity.

Now, Gregory wanders the multiverse, a **grumbling, tool-laden figure** draped in the weight of his divine burden. He carries a vast sack filled with maps, manuals, and an assortment of tools—each one essential for the never-ending task of dungeon maintenance. His presence is often marked by the **flicker of torches reigniting**, the **creak of mechanisms resetting**, or the **distant clatter of his tools** as he mutters under his breath about the incompetence of adventurers and the laziness of his divine employers.

To the rare few who encounter him, Gregory is dismissive, impatient, and utterly uninterested in conversation. He speaks in **short, blunt sentences**, as if every word is a coin he’d rather not spend. Yet, if pressed, he might—*might*—offer a hint about the dangers ahead. More often than not, however, his warnings pertain to entirely different dungeons, ones he has just left or is about to visit. His knowledge is vast, but his willingness to share it is limited, and his advice is as likely to be cryptic as it is to be useful.

Gregory’s existence is a paradox: a god who **hates his divinity**, a custodian who **resents his charges**, and a figure of **unmatched skill** in a role he never wanted. Yet, without him, the dungeons of the world would crumble, traps would fail, and the delicate balance of danger and order would collapse. And so, the Dungeon Gnome endures—**grumbling, working, and forever trapped in a job he can never quit**.

### A reluctant helper

Gregory is the embodiment of reluctant competence. Truly excelling at his job, but never feeling that he gets the true recognition he deserves, he performs his jumb grumbling and with annoyed speed.

When speaking, Gregory favors short, clipped sentences, as if every word is a coin he’d rather not spend. His voice is dry, his tone exasperated, and his patience nonexistent. He constantly fidgets with his tool belt, even when it’s already perfectly organized, and mumbles under his breath about "lazy liches" and "careless dragons." If a player attempts to help, he waves them off with a dismissive "No, no, no, you’ll just make it worse."

Gregory’s knowledge is vast but selectively shared. He knows everything about the dungeon he’s currently in, but his information about other dungeons is unreliable. When pressed for hints, roll a d6:

| D6 | Accuracy |
|:------------------|:------|
| 1-2                 | His information is accurate and relevant. |
| 3-4               | The information is accurate but pertains to a different dungeon. |
| 5-6               | The information is either completely wrong or deliberately misleading. |

On a 5–6, 
Though he cannot directly interfere with mortal affairs, Gregory can subtly influence dungeon mechanics to aid—or hinder—the party. A portcullis might "accidentally" fail to close, or a pit trap might reset just as the last party member crosses. These interventions are never overt; Gregory’s help is as grudging as his presence.

If ever forced to reveal his true divine form, Gregory’s appearance shifts dramatically. His small, gnome-like frame expands into a massive, stone-skinned figure with a beard made of clinking chains, a crown of rusted gears, and eyes like smoldering forge coals. He hates this form, and the transformation is accompanied by a groan of pure exasperation.

### Divine Traits

- **Everburning Torch.** Gregory’s presence causes all nonmagical torches within 60 feet to burn indefinitely, even in magical darkness or underwater.
- **Dungeon Sense.** He is always aware of the location and status of every trap, secret door, and structural weakness within 1 mile of his current location.
- **Reluctant Omniscience.** Gregory knows the layout of every dungeon he maintains, but he *hates* being asked about it. If forced to answer, he provides the information in the most unhelpful, cryptic, or roundabout way possible.

### Actions

- **Multiattack.** Gregory makes two **Tool Strike** attacks.
- **Tool Strike.** *Melee Weapon Attack:* +7 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target. *Hit:* 10 (2d6 + 3) bludgeoning damage plus 7 (2d6) force damage.
- **Maintenance Mode (Recharge 5–6).** Gregory waves his hands, and all traps within 30 feet reset to their original state. Creatures within the area must succeed on a DC 17 Dexterity saving throw or take 21 (6d6) force damage as mechanisms snap back into place.
- **Divine Grumble.** As a bonus action, Gregory mutters a complaint. Each creature of his choice within 30 feet must succeed on a DC 16 Wisdom saving throw or be **frustrated** until the end of its next turn. A frustrated creature has disadvantage on ability checks and attack rolls.

### Legendary Actions

Gregory can take 3 legendary actions, choosing from the options below. Only one legendary action option can be used at a time and only at the end of another creature’s turn.

- **Adjust Lever.** Gregory pulls a lever or turns a valve within 60 feet, activating or deactivating a dungeon mechanism.
- **Sigh of the Ages.** Gregory lets out a long-suffering sigh. Each creature within 10 feet must succeed on a DC 16 Constitution saving throw or be **deafened** until the end of its next turn.
- **Dungeon Step.** Gregory can teleport to any unnoccupied space within a dungeon that he is in, as long as he knows the layout.

## Adventure Hooks

1. **The Dungeon’s Custodian.** The party stumbles upon Gregory in the middle of resetting a deadly trap. He grumbles at them to leave, but if they persist, he might warn them about a *completely unrelated* dungeon’s deadly mimic infestation.
2. **A God’s Day Off.** Gregory has gone missing, and dungeons across the realm are falling into disrepair. The party is hired to find him—only to discover he’s hiding in a tavern, refusing to work until someone "fixes the *real* problem" (which turns out to be a bureaucratic nightmare involving a forgotten contract with a long-dead archdevil).
3. **The Wrong Dungeon.** Gregory accidentally gives the party a map to a dungeon *they weren’t supposed to enter*. Now, they’re trapped in a high-level deathtrap, and Gregory is *not* happy about the paperwork this will create.
4. **Divine Strike.** A rival deity has decided Gregory’s job is *too important* to be left to a "lazy gnome" and sends agents to replace him. Gregory, for once, is *motivated*—to get the party to help him keep his job.

## Magic Items

#### Paper Armor of Folding
*Wondrous item, Artifact (requires attunement)*
:
This knob is pretty nice. When attached to a door, it allows a user to
open that door with the strength of the nearest animal. For example, if
there is a cow nearby, the user will have the "strength of a cow" while
opening this door.

#### Gregory’s Omnikey Chain
*Wondrous Item, Legendary (requires attunement by Gregory or his explicit permission)*
:
A rusted iron ring bears an ever-shifting collection of keys—some tiny, some massive, some shaped like gears or bones. The keys clink together with a sound like distant machinery, and no two keys are ever the same when examined closely.

- **Omnifit.** While holding this keychain, you can use an action to flip through its keys. After 1d4 + 1 rounds of searching, you will always find the correct key for any door, lock, or sealed mechanism within the dungeon you are currently in. If the door lacks a keyhole, one will magically appear when the correct key is pressed against it.
- **Gregory’s Recall.** The keychain is magically bound to Gregory. After it has been used to open **three doors or mechanisms**, it vanishes from your possession and reappears on Gregory’s belt, wherever he may be. If you attempt to leave the dungeon with the keychain, it disappears immediately and returns to him.
- **Divine Grumble.** Each time you use the keychain, Gregory’s voice mutters in your ear: *"Took you long enough."* or *"Don’t lose that one."*

#### Satchel of Infinite Schematics
*Wondrous Item, Very Rare (requires attunement by Gregory or his explicit permission)*
:
This worn leather satchel is stuffed with rolled-up parchments, blueprints, and scribbled notes. The moment you reach inside, a single, relevant schematic for the trap, puzzle, or mechanism you currently face materializes in your hands.

- **Trap Insight.** As an action, you can pull a schematic from the satchel and attempt to decipher it. Roll a **d20** and add your Intelligence modifier:
  - **1–9:** The schematic is a blur of lines and symbols. You gain no useful information.
  - **10–14:** The schematic is partially legible. You learn **one piece of useful information** about the trap (e.g., a weak point, a hidden mechanism, or the general method of disarming it).
  - **15–19:** The schematic is clear. You learn **two pieces of information** and gain **advantage on your next ability check** related to the trap.
  - **20:** The schematic is perfectly detailed. You learn **all relevant information** about the trap, including how to disarm or bypass it without risk, and gain **advantage on all related checks** for the next hour.
- **Gregory’s Commentary.** If you roll a 1, Gregory’s voice grumbles from the satchel: *"Of course you can’t read it. It’s upside-down."* If you roll a 20, he mutters, *"Finally, someone with half a brain."*
- **Limited Access.** The satchel only produces schematics for traps, puzzles, or mechanisms **within the dungeon you are currently in**. If you attempt to use it outside a dungeon, it produces only a crumpled note reading: *"Not here. Stop wasting my time."*
